# General Cover Letter Reference

For any journal not specifically listed. Use the standard 4-paragraph formalism.

---

## 4-Paragraph Formalism

### Paragraph 1 — Title, Article Type, Background, and Question

> We submit [article type] entitled "[Title]". [Background — 1–2 sentences on context and what was previously known]. [Research question — what this study set out to answer, and why answering it matters].

### Paragraph 2 — What Was Done, Main Findings, Significance

> [Methods — one brief phrase]. [Main finding 1]. [Main finding 2 if present]. [Significance — what these findings change or enable].

### Paragraph 3 — Relevance to the Journal

> We believe the readers of [Journal] would be interested in this work because [specific argument: how it fulfils the journal's aims and scope, and why it matters to that readership].

### Paragraph 4 — Corresponding Author and Requirements

> Correspondence should be addressed to [name] at [email]. This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not currently under consideration by another journal.

---

## Rules

- Length: 200–400 words; never exceed one page.
- Paragraph 1 opening: never start with "We are pleased to submit", "We report", or a method.
- Paragraph 2 findings: be specific; avoid "novel", "important", "significant" without content.
- Paragraph 3 fit: must be specific to the named journal — name audience, scope, or a related paper.
- Paragraph 4: include only what the journal requires; omit ¶4 if no requirements apply and no corresponding author email is available.

---

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Use active voice throughout.
- State the research question as something that genuinely needed answering.
- Name the broader implication in ¶2 (outside the immediate subfield).

**Don't:**
- Restate the abstract word-for-word.
- Use hedging language ("may suggest", "could potentially").
- Include funding information or technical experimental detail.
