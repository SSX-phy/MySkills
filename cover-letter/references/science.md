# Science (AAAS) — Cover Letter Reference

Source: https://www.science.org/content/page/science-information-authors
## Mapping
- science, science aaas → `science`
---

## Status

Cover letter is **mandatory**. Uploaded as a PDF or .docx via the Related Documents tab at
https://cts.sciencemag.org

---

## Structure

Use the 4-paragraph formalism with ONE extra paragraph inserted between Paragraph1 and Paragraph2.

Final order: Paragraph1 → ParagraphSCIENCE → Paragraph2 → Paragraph3 → Paragraph4

---

## Paragraph 1 — Title, Article Type, Background, and Question

Standard formalism. **Critical addition**: Paragraph1 must pitch to a multidisciplinary, non-specialist
Science readership. Frame the background and question so a researcher from an adjacent field
understands why it matters — not just specialists.

---

## Paragraph SCIENCE — Colleague Reviewers and Ethics (insert after Paragraph1)

This paragraph is **mandatory** and is one of the most commonly missed elements.

**Colleague reviewer list** (required):
> "The following colleagues reviewed a draft of this manuscript prior to submission:
> [Name 1, Affiliation 1]; [Name 2, Affiliation 2]; [Name 3, Affiliation 3]."

Ask the user: *"Science requires a list of colleagues who reviewed a draft before submission.
Please provide 2–4 names with affiliations."* If the user cannot provide names, flag this
clearly in the draft — submitting without it signals poor preparation to editors.

**Ethics statements** (include only if applicable):
- Human studies: *"This study was approved by [Institution] IRB (protocol [number])
  and all participants provided informed consent."*
- Animal studies: *"All animal procedures were performed in accordance with [Institution]
  Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines."*

**Related submissions and prior editor discussions** (include if applicable):
> "A related manuscript has been submitted to [journal]." or
> "We had a pre-submission inquiry with [editor name] on [date]."

---

## Paragraph 2 — What Was Done, Main Findings, Significance

Standard formalism.

---

## Paragraph 3 — Relevance to the Journal

Emphasise cross-disciplinary relevance. Science's readership spans biology, chemistry, physics,
and earth sciences. Paragraph3 should name at least two fields the work bridges or speaks to.

---

## Paragraph 4 — Corresponding Author and Requirements

Include:
1. Correspondence line (standard).
2. Originality statement: *"This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under
   consideration by another journal."*
3. Data availability: *"All data are available at [repository/URL]."* (or note availability
   in the supplementary materials)

---

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Lead Paragraph1 with why the finding matters to a non-specialist Science reader.
- Include the colleague reviewer list — Science editors notice its absence.

**Don't:**
- Write for specialists only — Science's value is breadth.
- Omit IRB/IACUC statements if the study involves humans or animals.
