# Elsevier / Cell Press — Cover Letter Reference

Sources:
- https://www.elsevier.support/publishing/answer/what-should-be-included-in-a-cover-letter
- https://crosstalk.cell.com/blog/submitting-your-manuscript-write-the-right-cover-letter

Applies to: Elsevier journals generally, Cell, Cell Reports, Current Biology, Lancet,
and other Cell Press titles.

---
## Mapping
- elsevier, cell, lancet, cell press → `elsevier`


## Status

Cover letter is **mandatory** for most Elsevier journals (including all Cell Press titles).
Length: ideally ≤ 350 words; never exceed one page.

---

## Structure

Use the standard 4-paragraph formalism with the following modifications.

---

### Paragraph  1 — Title, Article Type, Background, and Question

**Cell-specific**: Paragraph 1 should open by reframing a prior assumption that the paper overturns.
Preferred framing:
> "[X] has long been assumed to [Y]. We show that it instead [Z]."

This is preferred over a neutral background statement when the paper contradicts prior consensus.
Use the standard formalism for papers that extend rather than overturn prior work.

---

### Paragraph  2 — What Was Done, Main Findings, Significance

Standard formalism.

---

### Paragraph  3 — Relevance to the Journal

Reference the journal's specific **Aims and Scope** or cite a concrete related paper the journal
has published. Generic statements ("your broad readership") are insufficient.

Ask the user in Step 5 for a specific journal connection if they have one.

---

### Paragraph  4 — Corresponding Author and Requirements

Paragraph 4 must include ALL of the following for Elsevier/Cell Press:

**1. Correspondence line** (standard).

**2. Originality declaration** (required):
> "This work is original and the manuscript is not currently under consideration for publication
> elsewhere."

**3. Conflicts of interest** (required — explicit declaration):
> "The authors declare no conflicts of interest." OR
> "The following conflicts of interest are disclosed: [details]."

Ask the user: *"Do you have any conflicts of interest to declare?"*

**4. AI-tool disclosure** (required — both in the cover letter AND in the manuscript):
> "The following AI tools were used in preparing this manuscript: [tool names].
> [Brief description of how they were used]."

Ask the user: *"Did you use any AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Grammarly) in preparing
this manuscript? If yes, name them and describe how."* If no: *"No AI tools were used in
preparing this manuscript."*

---

## What to Exclude from the Letter

Do NOT include in the cover letter — these go in the submission form separately:
- Funding information
- Reviewer suggestions (submitted via the journal's web form, not the letter)
- Full author declarations beyond AI and COI

---

## Cell Press Additional Guidance

- Be genuine: Cell editors say the cover letter should reflect real enthusiasm for the work,
  not formulaic language.
- Be humble: avoid claiming priority or making comparisons to other labs' work without
  full justification.
- Keep it focused: do not write a second abstract — synthesise, don't repeat.

---

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Include COI and AI disclosure — their absence can delay processing.
- Reference a specific Elsevier/Cell paper or the journal's scope in Paragraph 3.

**Don't:**
- Include reviewer suggestions in this letter.
- Include funding sources.
- Exceed one page.
