Sources:

## Mapping
- prl, physical review letters → `prl`
---

## Status

Cover letter: optional separate document.
100-word justification: **mandatory** — a dedicated field in the APS submission portal, separate
from the cover letter. Always generate both.

---

## Cover Letter Structure

Use the standard 4-paragraph formalism.

###  3 — Relevance to the Journal

Must argue for **broad physics significance**, not just subfield interest. PRL's readership spans
all of physics. Paragraph 3 should answer: why does a condensed-matter physicist (or particle physicist,
or AMO physicist) care about this result?

### Paragraph  4 — Corresponding Author and Requirements

Include:
1. Correspondence line.
2. Note any **joint submissions** (the same result submitted simultaneously to another journal).
3. Note **Physical Review submission history** if the paper was previously submitted to any
   Physical Review journal and rejected/transferred.

## Separate Output: 100-Word Portal Justification

After completing the four-paragraph cover letter, generate the following **as a separate block**:

```
=== PRL SUBMISSION PORTAL ===
Paste the text below into the "Justification" field in the APS submission form.
This field is read by the divisional editor before they read the manuscript.
Strictly ≤ 100 words. Do not include the header in the word count.
=============================

[Justification text here]
```

### Rules for the 100-word justification

The justification must cover the **three I's**:

1. **Impact** — How does this work advance the field, open a new research direction,
   or solve a critical outstanding problem?
2. **Innovation** — What specifically does this work do that previous work did not?
   Name the prior limitation and what this paper overcomes.
3. **Interest** — Why will a broad PRL readership (across all of physics) find this compelling?

**Format**: Open with the result (one sentence). Then address each of the three I's.
Write as an **argument**, not a summary. Do NOT merely restate the journal's acceptance criteria.

**Bad example**: "This paper makes a significant advance with broad appeal and is innovative."
**Good example**: "We show that [X] contradicts the long-standing prediction of [Y].
This overturns a theoretical assumption made across [field], opening a path toward [implication].
The result follows from [method], which we validate against [independent check].
The finding will interest researchers in [subfield A] and [subfield B]."

Count the words strictly. If over 100, trim — never exceed the limit.

---

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Treat the 100-word justification as the most important part of the submission — it gates
  peer review.
- Make the three I's concrete: name the prior work displaced, name the new capability gained.

**Don't:**
- Confuse the 100-word justification with the cover letter — they go in different fields.
- Let the justification exceed 100 words.
- Write the justification as a mini-abstract — it must be an editorial argument.
